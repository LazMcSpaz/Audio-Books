Indeed, these three levels of the mind may be likened to a kitchen, the foreconscious being the cupboard, the fringe of consciousness is the table, and the focus of consciousness the mixing basin, and the ideas from the three levels may be represented by the ingredients of the pudding, some of which are put away in the cupboard, some lie ready to the hand upon the table and others are actually in the mixing basin being stirred. Those on the table, like the ideas in the fringe of consciousness, lie ready to the cook's hand but she is not dealing with them at the moment; those in the cupboard, (the foreconscious) are out of sight, but she knows they are there and can get them if she wants them, but it is only those that are in the basin, the focus of consciousness, that she is actually at work upon. To the average man these three levels constitute all there is of his mind, he has no conception of the strange hinterland lying behind the narrow strip of civilised coast, yet it is here that the springs of his being take their rise, and it is the discovery and exploration of this hinterland which has been the great contribution of modern psychology to the sum of human knowledge.
